CANARY

A first-vent detector for the lithium battery charging in your hallway. It listens for a failing cell's solvent vapour and CO₂, before there is any smoke.

Try to set it off

This runs the detector from the firmware (detect.c, ported line for line) against a simulated pack and room. Spray cleaner, breathe on it, warm the room: each is one kind of evidence and gets amber. Vent a cell and gas and CO₂ arrive together.

The real firmware, recorded

A vent at 400 s. Alarm 7.6 s later.

Not the port above: this is the compiled canary.elf running on simavr's ATmega328P, with virtual Sensirion sensors answering its I²C commands with their datasheet framing and CRCs. The traces are what the firmware printed on its serial pin, one line a second.

Order one

From PCBWay, assembled

  1. Upload production/canary-gerbers.zip: 2 layers, 1.6 mm, 6/6 mil, 0.3 mm holes, yellow mask, black silk, ENIG.
  2. Add assembly, top side only, with canary-bom-pcbway.csv and canary-cpl.csv. Ask for no washing on the Sensirion parts.
  3. Ask them to flash canary.hex through the Tag-Connect pads, fuses E2 / D9 / FD.
  4. Plug it into any USB-C charger. Green blinks through a 90 s warm-up, then breathes.

Limits

What it is not, yet

Not a certified safety device. Keep your smoke alarm.

The thresholds follow how venting cells are known to behave, but no real cell has vented beside this board yet. That test is next, and the numbers in detect.h may move.

No battery backup: when the outlet is dead, so is Canary.

No board has been assembled yet. Status: designed and verified in simulation.
