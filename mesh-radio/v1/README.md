# Mesh Radio — v1

**A LoRa mesh radio on one narrow board, built only from parts a US or
allied factory can buy and assemble.** It does what the RAK WisBlock does in
OffGrid's mesh nodes today (Meshtastic over LoRa, set up from the phone over
Bluetooth), on a single board shaped like a Heltec V4. It also has a
transmit amplifier that goes past the legal 1 W for licensed or experimental
use, a locking serial port for the Cairn drone, and protection on every
input.

> **Status: layout placed, routing in progress (20 Oct 2026). Not yet
> built.**
>
> - **Board:** 58 × 25.4 mm, 6 layers (JLCPCB JLC06161H-2116B: In1 solid
>   ground, In2–In4 signals with ground fill). 250 parts, 243 of them
>   assembled, on both sides.
> - **Routing is not finished.** There is no DRC result, no Gerbers, no
>   BOM/CPL files and no renders yet.
> - **Simulations** (power, radio, heat) ran on the schematic and the placed
>   design on 19 Oct. They run again on the routed board.
> - **Firmware:** the Meshtastic variant builds. Nothing has run on
>   hardware.
> - **Owner decisions, 19 Oct:** keep 3 W and fix the heat (a gap pad to a
>   metal enclosure, nine filled vias under the amplifier); 6 layers; the
>   designer picks the new parts; charging at 300 mA.
>
> Nothing is ordered or measured. Every test and its result:
> [`TESTS.md`](TESTS.md).

## Who it is for

- **Cairn nodes.** These are the mesh nodes the Cairn drone drops: on a
  1.5–2 m pole or on a treetop.
  - Each node weighs under 300 g.
  - A node runs on one LiPo cell for days to weeks.
  - In the sun, it sits in a sealed box at 55–60 °C.
  - The node is live on the mesh while the drone carries it. The drone's
    onboard computer (Jetson Orin Nano) reads signal strength from it over
    a cable to pick the drop point.
- **Any OffGrid node** that uses a RAK WisBlock today.
- **A defence build later.** If a contract needs NDAA-compliant radios built
  in the US or allied countries, this design is the starting point. It
  should need only minor changes, not months of work.

### Protection: it must not burn out

| # | Requirement | How it is checked | v1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| X1 | Battery plugged in backwards: no damage | Simulation; tested on built boards | ✅ in simulation, all six cases at −40 to 85 °C (20 Oct, with a 4.7 V clamp on the polarity-check FET's gate). One sequence (a right-way cell pulled out under external power, then a reversed one) still draws 20–21 A for 0.2 ms until the protector trips, inside every part's pulse rating; firmware clears that state when the processor runs. Bench test (`sim/reverse/results.txt`). |
| X2 | Short circuit on any output pin or the battery path: current is limited and the board recovers | eFuse / current-limit simulation | ✅ in simulation on every rail. A short on the cell trips the protector in 0.3 ms; restart on the cell alone is a bench test (`sim/shorts`). |
| X3 | Wrong or surging input on USB, solar or the drone port, up to at least 20 V and with plug-in spikes: no damage | Hot-plug and overvoltage simulation | ✅ in simulation at every temperature: 20 V faults, a 12 V panel, reversed supplies, a 5 → 20 V step (`sim/hotplug`) |
| X4 | A bad or weak battery (high internal resistance, deep discharge to 2.5 V, a 4.35 V "HV" cell, the cell pulled out while on solar): no damage, clean restart | Simulation set | ⏳ worn and cold cells pass with the firmware caps (`sim/burst`); a 4.35 V cell is inside the protector's rating (`sim/derating`). Deep discharge to 2.5 V and a cell pulled out on solar were not run as their own cases. |
| X5 | Unprotected cells are protected on the board (over-discharge, over-charge, over-current) | Protection IC in the circuit | ✅ BQ29700 protector in the circuit; its trip times simulated (`sim/shorts`) |
| X6 | Static (ESD) protection on USB, the antenna port and every external pin | Parts in the circuit | ✅ USB, drone and GPS plugs (TPD4E05U06) and the antenna (47 nH shunt) (`src/circuit.py`). ❌ none on the expansion header's pins (header fitted only when needed). |
| X7 | Never damaged by transmitting with no antenna attached (field mistake) | PA ruggedness rating and power limit | ❌ in simulation: the amplifier passes (9.1:1 against its 10:1 rating), but the antenna switch sees 34.6–50.7 V against 31.6 V, and one inductor overheats at 4.0 V (`sim/rf`). ## Testing: what can and cannot be proven before building

Proven in simulation before ordering:

- power surges and plug-in spikes;
- short circuits and reversed battery;
- weak, bad and over-voltage batteries;
- solar input;
- 3 W bursts on a worn battery;
- heat at 60 °C with a message storm;
- filter harmonics;
- trace impedance.

Proven only on built boards:

- the exact output power;
- antenna matching;
- receive sensitivity;
- real current draw.

The plan is a small test batch measured on a bench with a spectrum
analyser, side by side with a RAK. The full list, with each result so far:
[`TESTS.md`](TESTS.md).

## How to rebuild the schematic

```
mesh-radio/v1/tools/kd python3 src/sch.py     # from mesh-radio/v1, in the pinned KiCad 10.0.7 image
```

It redraws `schematic/` from `src/circuit.py`, checks that KiCad's netlist
equals the circuit, runs KiCad's ERC and exports the PDF. Rebuilds are
byte-identical.
