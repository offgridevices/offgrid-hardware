## Findings against ARCHITECTURE.md as written

1. **Pre-charge cannot reach 75 % with the planned ~22 Ω** when a 10-15 W digital VTX is on the drone (it traps VOUT at the VTX's 6 V UVLO for every 8S-14S pack; 10 Ω still fails 8S-9S below ~3.85 V/cell). 8.2 Ω reaches 75 % for 8S-14S at ≥ 3.7 V/cell; packs that still stall at the UVLO (2S-6S, near-empty 8S) are closed by the slow main switch well inside SOA, so one bank replaces the SPEC's two (2 Ω / 22 Ω).
2. **'Q1 body diode blocks a reversed battery' is not true** for the common-source pair: Q1's body diode conducts and drags the common source (and every TPS48111/INA290 pin on the input) to −60 V. Q2 blocks the power path, but the controller, current amp and pre-charge driver do not survive.
3. **The pre-charge P-FET's body diode** back-feeds whenever VOUT > VIN (battery on the drone side puts ≈ 60 V on the input connector pins; a higher pack on the drone side charges the input pack at ≈ 1 A with everything 'off'), and forward-conducts through RPRE for a reversed input pack with a drone attached. A series Schottky (Dpre) fixes it.
4. **A reversed pack on the drone side** pulls SRC to VOUT + 0.7 V; the TPS48111 tolerates −30 V, i.e. only up to 6S.
5. **Ceramic-only input capacitance rings to > 100 V** on hot plug and on short-circuit turn-off; a damped input network is required, plus an output RC snubber to keep SRC above −30 V after a short turn-off at low cell counts.
6. **Peak comparator:** INA290A2 saturates at ≈ 64 A (25 A × 4 = 100 A unreachable) and the comparator output must be latched.

**Chosen RPRE = 8.2 Ω** (largest value meeting (a)). None of the requested values 10-47 Ω meets (a) at the low end (8S below ~4.2 V/cell): the binding limit is not the 75 % point but getting *past the VTX UVLO*. At VOUT = 6 V the VTX switches on and wants P/6 V; if (VIN − 6 V)/R is less than that, VOUT is trapped at the UVLO (the low equilibrium of a constant-power load). Condition: R < 6 V·(VIN − 6 V)/P_total (8S at 3.7 V/cell with 15 W + 1 W FC → R < 8.9 Ω; 14S LiHV → R < 21 Ω). The second condition, an operating point above 0.75·VIN, needs R ≤ 0.1875·VIN²/P and is less strict.

Why an output snubber: when Q1/Q2 open, the battery-lead and drone-lead currents keep flowing in series through Q1's Coss and the input network; the drone side swings negative and the common source follows it through Q2's body diode. Without anything on the output, SRC goes below the TPS48111's −30 V SRC-to-GND rating at low cell counts (the 'alt: no output snubber' rows) and Q1 approaches avalanche with long drone leads. A Schottky from PG to VOUT (TI's suggestion) clamps best but would short a reversed battery on the drone side unless Q4 (case 5) is fitted. An RC snubber on VOUT (ceramic, non-polar, survives a reversed drone battery) of 10uF effective at 60 V + 0.22 Ω keeps every case inside the limits. The damped input network matters as much: with ceramic only (input B) the input side rings to > 100 V and Q1 avalanches.

Above ≈ 100 V (two packs of ≥ 12S) Q1 and the 100 V aux OR diode break down and conduct tens of amps. No 100 V-rated part can block 122 V; only 150 V FETs/diodes or the user not doing it fix this. With Q4 the AUX-driven gate turns Q4 on and does not help.


Without Q4, a reversed pack on the drone side drags the common source to VOUT + 0.7 V through Q2's body diode. That is inside the TPS48111's −30 V rating only up to 6S (−26.1 V LiHV); 7S LiHV (−30.5 V) and above exceed it. Q4 (drone-side B− FET, gate from AUX) leaves the reversed pack with no return path, so nothing on the board sees it. Q4 also makes a PG→VOUT Schottky clamp legal (case 3 alternative). Cost: one more 5×6 FET in the main current path (+≈3 mΩ; total through-path ≈ 1 + 4 × 3 mΩ + connectors, still ≤ 20 mΩ). Q4 is a controlled switch, not a passive diode: normal drone return current and a reversed drone-side pack both flow DNEG → PG, so Q4 must be ON (gate from AUX) whenever the board is powered and relies on being unpowered (AUX dead) when only a reversed pack is present. If Q4 is not wanted, the board survives reversed drone-side packs only up to 6S and must say so.

## Case 7 — 3 V probe classification

Probe: 3.0 V source → 1 kΩ for 100 ms, then 100 Ω for 300 ms → 100 V Schottky → VOUT; the ADC reads VOUT through 100 kΩ (clamped, 10 nF) at 1 kHz. The board's own VOUT capacitance (10 µF output snubber, Q2 Coss) is present and subtracted. Main switch and pre-charge off, 6S on the input (1S for the whoop case).

Firmware features: V1 = mean VOUT at 90-100 ms (1 kΩ), V2 = mean at 390-400 ms (100 Ω), s2 = slope over 350-400 ms, R = V/I with I = (3.0 V − Vf − V)/Rs (Vf = 0.25 V / 0.35 V nominal), C = ∫I dt / V up to V = 1.5 V.

## Case 9 — Peak comparator timing

Path: 1 mΩ shunt → INA290A2 (50 V/V; 1.1 MHz, 2 V/µs, output ≤ 3.2 V) → push-pull comparator (TLV3201 class, 40 ns) → set-only latch (74LVC1G74 class, 5 ns; cleared by the MCU) → AND3 with MCU_EN and the hall guard (74LVC1G11, 4 ns) → 1 kΩ → TPS48111 INP; INP→PD 1 µs (datasheet max) → PD 0.69 Ω pulls the gates. The comparator and gate delays are lumped as a 45 ns RC deglitch. 6S pack, board ON, fault at the drone end of the leads. TPS48111 SCP (80 A) stays active.

Pass: from the moment the FET current crosses the set trip level to INP low < 5 µs; for a hard short also from fault onset to INP low < 5 µs.

The trip-level-to-INP time is set by the INA290: its 2 V/µs slew and 1.1 MHz pole add ≈ 0.3-0.6 µs, so the whole comparator path is ≈ 0.4-0.7 µs, comfortably inside 5 µs; the TPS48111's own INP→PD delay (≤ 1 µs) then dominates the gate turn-off. Two consequences: (1) with INA290A2 (50 V/V on 3.3 V) the output saturates at ≈ 64 A, so the peak setting must be capped at 60 A (25 A × 4 = 100 A is not reachable; the 80 A SCP covers it); (2) the comparator must **latch** (or drive a timer-break input that latches): without a latch INP returns high as soon as the current falls and the switch re-closes into the fault every few µs. For a resistive fault behind a big bulk capacitor the FET current only rises as the capacitor discharges, so onset → trip is long (that is physics, not latency).

## Open risks

1. **TPS48111 is behavioural.** SCP propagation, INP→PD delay and PU/PD peak-current limits come from the datasheet tables, not a TI model; confirm short-circuit cut-off and gate overshoot on the bench (case 3 worst-case margins are only a few volts on Q1 Vds and on SRC).
2. **FET model is generic (VDMOS), SOA/EAS are assumed numbers.** Re-run case 1(b) and case 3 against the chosen part's SOA and Zth curves once the part number is fixed.
3. **Architecture change needed for reversed packs** (cases 4/5): as drawn, a reversed input pack drives the TPS48111/INA290 pins far below their ratings (amps flow), the pre-charge FET back-feeds both ways, and a reversed drone-side pack ≥ 7S exceeds the TPS48111 SRC −30 V rating. Q3, Q4 and Dpre fix the single faults but add two 5×6 FETs (≈ +6 mΩ, ≈ $2 BOM). Q3/Q4 must be logic-level (Rds(on) specified at 4.5 V) so 1S packs enhance them; the sim used a standard-level model.
4. **Double fault** (pack on the input plus a reversed pack on the drone side, ≥ 12S each) exceeds 100 V across the off switch; not survivable with 100 V parts.
5. **Pre-charge resistor:** single 8.2 Ω bank replaces the SPEC's 2 Ω / 22 Ω pair; confirm a through-hole part with ≥ 30 J single-pulse capability from the vendor's pulse curve. Firmware must enforce the 15 J per-attempt energy budget and cool-down from the RPRE NTC.
6. **VTX model** (full power the instant VOUT crosses 6 V) is pessimistic for VTXs with soft-start and optimistic for ones with a higher UVLO; RPRE ≤ 6·(VIN−6 V)/P is the rule to re-check if a 20-25 W VTX must be supported on 8S.
7. **Input damper is an aluminium electrolytic** (height ~10 mm, ESR spread 0.2-0.6 Ω, ESR rises when cold). It is only safe because Q3 removes reverse voltage; the all-ceramic option E is the fallback.
8. **Output snubber needs ≈ 5 × 4.7 µF / 100 V 1210** to reach 10 µF at 60 V (6.8 µF is the minimum that passed, with < 2 V margin); it adds to the drone capacitance the probe reports (firmware subtracts it) and charges through RPRE.
9. **INA290A2 saturates at ≈ 64 A**: the peak limit must be capped at 60 A (25 A × 4 is not reachable); the 80 A SCP covers above. The peak comparator must latch.
10. **SCP threshold tolerance** (35-45 mV at the 40 mV point, ±12 %) puts the 80 A trip between ≈ 70 and 90 A; motor-test transients above 70 A would nuisance-trip.
11. **Lead and layout parasitics** (150-300 nH leads, 0.3 nH shunt, ideal hot-plug contact without arcing or bounce) are estimates; measure the real harness.
12. **Off-state VOUT offset:** leakage into the common source flows through Q2's body diode into VOUT; at 25 °C the model gives ≈ 0.1 V on an open output, but hot FET leakage (up to 100 µA at 125 °C) on the 1 MΩ VOUT divider could fake the 'VOUT > 1 V, drone side already has power' check. Keep the VOUT divider ≤ 100 kΩ total or qualify that check with the probe response.
