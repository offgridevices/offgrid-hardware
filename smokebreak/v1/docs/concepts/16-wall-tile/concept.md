# 16 Wall Tile

**Pitch:** a magnetic tile that lives at eye level on a toolbox, bench leg or pegboard. Power is a light switch (up is on, down is off), and every lead hangs straight down, off the bench top.

**Form & size:** 72 × 120 × 20 mm, ≈ 160 g. CNC 6061 (die-cast later) with a glass face. Four N52 magnets and a rubber back pad hold ≈ 3 kg in shear on steel. It also has a keyhole for a screw, and a printed plate for pegboard.

**Boards:** one, 66 × 112 mm, vertical. The power path is at the bottom next to the connectors; warm air rises out of a top seam, like a chimney. The logic and UI are above it. Everything is on the bottom edge: XT60PW + XT30PW male (left), USB-C (middle) and two 25 cm drone leads in clamped grommets (right).

**UX:** **Power** is a momentary rocker inside the Beacon Ring that springs back to the middle. Up: check, then on. Down: off, always. **Bind** and **Limit** are two wide keys. The 2.0" IPS shows the setting and asks "Props off? Press Limit again" for 20 A. First use: stick it up, battery lead up into the bottom, drone leads down to the drone, push the switch up. Noob-proof: everyone knows a light switch; down means stop without looking. It answers pain points 6, 3 and 7 best.

**Cost delta:** ≈ +$6 (CNC body, glass, IPS, magnets, rocker, longer leads).

**Risks:** the magnets could upset a drone's compass during calibration, so keep it 25 cm away. A yanked lead pulls in shear (the rubber pad resists that). It needs a vertical surface; a lean-back stand is an accessory.
