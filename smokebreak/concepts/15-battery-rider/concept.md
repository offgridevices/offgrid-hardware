# 15 Battery Rider

**Pitch:** a machined saddle that straps onto the LiPo with the strap you already use. Battery and stopper become one brick: nothing dangles, nothing drags, and you can carry it with the drone.

**Form & size:** 81 × 40 × 15 mm top with 8 mm skirts that hug the pack, ≈ 70 g. CNC 6061, black anodised, glass top, silicone grip pad underneath. A 22 mm strap tunnel runs under the face, so the strap never covers it. It fits packs 33–40 mm wide (4S/6S, 1100–2200 mAh). Smaller packs and 1S whoops (BT2.0 adapter) simply lie beside it.

**Boards:** one, 76 × 34 mm, 2 oz. The top carries a 1.3" OLED, the ring LEDs and Bind. The bottom carries the power path and logic. The Limit rocker uses two right-angle switches on the front edge. XT60PW + XT30PW (male, with a rib) are on the battery end (−X), the two drone leads leave clamped grommets at +X, and USB-C is on the rear flank.

**UX:** **Power** sits in the Beacon Ring on top. **Limit** is a − / + rocker on the flank: it steps down or up and never wraps, so you can't land on 20 A by cycling. 20 A needs + held for 1 s and "Props off?" on screen. **Bind** is a small key on top. First use: strap it on, battery lead into the end, drone leads to the drone, press Power. The short battery loop can only reach one place, which makes it noob-proof. It answers pain points 7, 8 and 4 best.

**Cost delta:** ≈ +$5 (CNC saddle, glass, rocker, pad).

**Risks:** it sits on a LiPo. The FETs go under the aluminium top (the heat sink), with an insulating pad below, an NTC and the 30 s window. Someone may try to fly with it: "Bench only" is printed on it, and the 10 A / 30 s limits would cut out in flight. Pack sizes vary.
