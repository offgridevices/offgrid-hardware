# 03 Lantern

**Pitch:** stands upright on the bench. A glowing glass band shows the status in 360°, readable across the room. Turn the knurled crown for Limit and press its centre for Power.

**Form & size:** Ø46 × 98 mm on a Ø52 × 7 mm stainless base, about 230 g (110 g of it ballast, so the leads can't tip it).
- Anodised aluminium tube with a flat window for the screen.
- A frosted glass halo band and a knurled aluminium crown.
- Batch 1: CNC with an MJF chassis. Later: impact-extruded tube.

**Boards (2):**
- **A, main:** 38 × 70 mm, vertical. The power path sits low, with the FETs and resistors on a gap pad over the stainless base, which is the heatsink. The MCU and 1.47" display sit high.
- XT60PW + XT30PW male are on the left edge, stacked with a rib between them. The leads are on the right edge. USB-C is on the back.
- **B, crown:** Ø40, carries Power, the ring LEDs and an AS5600 magnetic encoder that reads a magnet in the crown (contactless).
- A and B are joined by an 8-way FFC.

**UX:**
- **Power:** press the crown's centre.
- **Limit:** turn the crown. AUTO, 1, 2, 5 and 10 click into place. 20 A needs push-and-turn, like a medicine cap: props off.
- **Bind:** a stainless button under the screen.
- **First use:** 1 Battery in, 2 Drone in, 3 Press the top.
- **Pains answered (SPEC §2):** best on 6 (status across the room) and 4 (a real dial).

**Cost delta:** about +$9 (tube and crown +$4, base +$1.5, encoder +$0.7, IPS +$1.2, halo +$0.8, second board +$0.6).

**Risks:**
- Tall: it can topple if the base is cut.
- Getting the crown seal and detents right.
- More parts to assemble.

**Scores:** Premium 5 · Noob-proof 5 · Bench practicality 4 · Build quality 5 · Uniqueness 4
