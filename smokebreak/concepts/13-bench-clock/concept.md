# 13 Bench Clock

**Pitch:** a heavy machined wedge whose screen faces you like a desk clock. Power is one full-width bar you tap (or slap to stop), and every cable leaves from the back.

**Form & size:** 84 × 52 × 50 mm, face tilted 63°, ≈ 210 g. CNC 6061, bead-blasted and black anodised, with a glass face (Bone print on the back of the glass) and a full rubber foot. Batch 1: CNC (or MJF nylon + steel weight); later die-cast.

**Boards:** two. The power board (64 × 36 mm, 2 oz) lies on the floor and is padded to the aluminium base, which is the heat sink. XT60PW + XT30PW (male, with a rib between them), USB-C and the drone-lead grommets sit on its back edge. The face board (72 × 38 mm) sits on the 63° plane and carries a 1.9" IPS, the ring LEDs, Bind/Limit, the MCU and the beeper. A 24-pin FPC joins them.

**UX:** **Power** is the bar: tap to check then power on, tap again for off. During a 20 A test the bar is a huge kill target. **Bind** and **Limit** are machined keys on the face. Limit cycles, and 20 A asks for a second press on screen. First use: battery into the back, drone leads to the drone, tap the bar. Noob-proof: the control that matters is the biggest, the screen always faces you, no lead crosses the face. It answers pain points 6, 7 and 3 best.

**Cost delta:** ≈ +$7 (CNC body, glass, IPS, bar, FPC).

**Risks:** a dropped tool on the bar starts a (harmless) check, so 3 N actuation in a recess. The back panel is busy. A wedge packs less well.
