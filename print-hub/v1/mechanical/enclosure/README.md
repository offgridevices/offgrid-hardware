# Print Hub v1 enclosure

A two-part 3D-printed box for the Print Hub v1 board (86 x 50 x 1.6 mm): a
**base** tray with the standoffs and a **lid** that carries every opening,
plus four printed **feet**. Everything is generated by `enclosure.py`
(build123d 0.13), which also runs a fit check against the board and its
connectors.

![Assembled and exploded](printhub-preview.png)
![Top, back, underside and the lid's heat pads](printhub-views.png)

| | |
|---|---|
| Outer size | **90.6 x 53.2 x 18.0 mm** (w x d x h), 21.5 mm tall on its printed feet |
| Walls / top plate | 2.0 mm. Under the top plate the lid steps down to just above the parts (see "Heat") |
| Floor | 1.0 mm under the board, 4.0 mm under the USB-A pins. The base's bottom face is flat |
| Board clearance | 0.3 mm all round |
| Fasteners | **2 x M3 x 12 socket head cap screw (ISO 4762 / DIN 912) + 2 x M3 x 4 mm heat-set inserts**. The board's front edge is clamped by the case itself (see "Holding the board") |
| Material | base 22.7 cm³, lid 30.4 cm³, feet 1.5 cm³ (about 29 g, 39 g and 2 g of PETG at 100 %) |

## Files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `enclosure.py` | The parametric model. Every dimension is a named constant at the top with a plain-English comment |
| `printhub-base.stl` | Base, **in print orientation** (floor on the bed) |
| `printhub-lid.stl` | Lid, **in print orientation** (top face on the bed) |
| `printhub-feet.stl` | The four feet, upright |
| `printhub-enclosure-step.zip` | All parts in their assembled position (+Z up, +Y towards the back / USB-C side, board bottom face at Z = 0) |
| `printhub-preview.png` | Assembled iso view and exploded view |
| `printhub-views.png` | Top (front edge down), back (USB-C side), underside, and the lid from below |
| `case_gaps.json` | The air gaps between the board and the case, for `sim/thermal/board_heat.py` |
| `fit_board.py` | Fit check of the real assembled board model in the case |

Rebuild (about 2 minutes, most of it the fit check):

```sh
cd print-hub/v1 && tools/kd python3 mechanical/enclosure/enclosure.py
```

`--check-only` runs only the fit check. `--no-check` only builds and exports.
`case_gaps.json` is written on every run. The exit code is 1 if any fit-check
line fails. The output is reproducible: the STEP export has a fixed timestamp,
so the same constants give the same files.

The script reads every part's courtyard from the routed board
(`../../print-hub.kicad_pcb`, through pcbnew in the same image) on each run.
`PART_HEIGHTS` gives each footprint type's height. A footprint that is not in
that table stops the build.

### Switches at the top of `enclosure.py`

| Constant | Default | Other choice |
|---|---|---|
| `FASTENER` | `'insert'`: Ø4.0 x 5.0 mm pockets for M3 x 4 heat-set inserts | `'selftap'`: Ø2.6 x 5.5 mm pilot holes (use this for resin) |
| `LED_WINDOW` | `'hole'`: Ø2.5 mm through-holes | `'thin'`: 0.4 mm skin left in the top plate |
| `BUTTON_STYLE` | `'pin'`: Ø3 mm paperclip holes | `'flexure'`: printed cantilever plungers. **Does not fit this layout**: the status LED window falls on the BOOT tongue and the BOOT label on a screw head. The fit check fails it |
| `FRONT_PRESS` | `True`: the lid's front wall presses on the board between the USB-A ports | `False` leaves a 0.1 mm gap there (the board can then rock) |
| `BRIDGE_LAYER` | 0.2 | set it to your layer height (see "Printing") |

## Printing

All parts print **without supports**, on FDM and on resin, in these orientations:

* **Base: floor on the bed**, open side up.
* **Lid: top face on the bed**, open side up. The engraved text and the LED
  windows are on the bed face, so they come out crisp. The heat pads grow up
  from the top plate, and every step in them is vertical. Every opening in the
  lid walls (USB-A notches, USB-C recesses, side vents) is open at the lid's
  free edge, so nothing in the walls has to bridge.
* **Feet: upright.** They are plain Ø10.4 x 4.5 mm cylinders.

The fit check (section 9) lists every downward-facing face in the print
orientation. There are only three kinds, all deliberate:
* base: the ceilings of the four foot recesses, 10.6 mm round bridges 1 mm off the bed;
* lid: the floors of the engraved letters, letter-stroke-wide bridges 0.6 mm off the bed;
* lid: the counterbore floors. These use the stepped "bridging layer" trick:
  one layer bridges the 6.2 mm counterbore leaving a 3.4 mm slot, the next layer
  bridges the slot leaving a 3.4 mm square, and then the round hole carries on.
  It needs no supports and no drilling.

### FDM (0.4 mm nozzle)

* **Material: PETG** (or ASA/ABS). The board puts about 2.5 W into the box,
  and PLA can creep under the screw clamp once it gets warm.
* **Print the lid at 100 % infill** (or with enough perimeters that the pads
  come out solid). The pads carry the heat from the parts to the top plate,
  and the heat simulation assumes solid PETG. The base's 5 mm floor is better
  solid too.
* Layer height 0.2 mm (0.16 mm for crisper text; then set `BRIDGE_LAYER = 0.16` and rebuild).
* 5 perimeters, 5 top and 5 bottom layers.
* Supports off, no brim.
* Elephant-foot compensation of about 0.15 mm. Both parts have their outer
  edges on the bed, and the lid also has its text there.
* Use an Arachne-style slicer (PrusaSlicer 2.6+, Cura 5, Orca) or enable
  "detect thin walls". The front skin in the base is 0.6 mm and the frame
  around each USB-C is 0.7 mm (see "Design notes").

### Resin

* Same orientations, directly on the build plate, no supports. Neither part
  traps resin: both open towards the vat.
* Use a tough or ABS-like resin at 0.05 mm layers. Standard resin is brittle
  around the screw bosses.
* **Heat-set inserts do not work in resin** (it does not melt). Set
  `FASTENER = 'selftap'` and rebuild, or glue the inserts into the 4.0 mm pockets with epoxy.
* The lid is now mostly solid (30 cm³). Hollowing it would defeat the heat pads.

| Qty | Part | Notes |

## Assembly

1. Clean up the prints. Check that the thin front skin of the base is intact.
2. **Inserts**: press the two inserts into the standoffs (back-left corner, and
   the middle one beside the button pillar) with a soldering iron (about
   220-240 °C for PETG). Make them flush with the standoff top. One standing
   proud makes the board rock, so sand it flush if needed.
3. **Feet**: glue the four printed feet into the recesses on the bottom.
4. **Board**: lower it straight into the base, components up, USB-A side to the
   front (the side with the thin skin). It rests on the two standoffs, the
   ledge under its front edge and the pillar under the buttons.
5. **Lid**: lower it straight down. The notches in its front wall pass over the
   USB-A shells, the two halves of each USB-C recess meet, and the front wall
   comes down onto the board between the ports.
6. **Screws**: drive the two M3 x 12 screws through the lid and the board into
   the inserts. Tighten them snug (about 0.4 N·m), not hard: they clamp the board
   between the lid's bosses and the standoffs, and pull the lid's front wall
   down onto the board's front edge.
7. Check that a USB-A and a USB-C cable each seat fully, and that RST clicks with a paperclip.

## Design notes

**Coordinates.** All positions in the script use the board's KiCad
coordinates: millimetres from the board centre, x to the right, y towards the
front (USB-A) edge, z up from the board's bottom face. KiCad's y points down the
screen, so the 3D model uses Y = -y. Building straight in KiCad (x, y) would
have produced a mirror-image box.

**The split** is a single flat plane 0.1 mm above the board's top face. The base
is a shallow tray. The lid carries everything above the board, and all its
connector openings are notches open at its bottom edge, so it lowers straight
down over the connectors.

**Holding the board.** The board has only two M3 holes: the back-left corner
(-39.5, -21.5) and beside the ESP32 module (7, -2.5). Each one is clamped
between a lid boss (Ø6.8 mm, inside the Ø7 keep-clear circle) and a base
standoff (Ø7 mm). Two screws alone would let the board tip about the line
through them, so the case also clamps the whole front edge:
* the base's front wall is a ledge under the board's front strip (y 23.9-25.0),
  touching the board like the standoffs do;
* the lid's front wall comes down onto the board's top face between the USB-A
  notches and at both front corners (six pads, 1.3-1.6 x 1.1 mm). Nothing but
  the receptacles is on that strip, and they sit in the notches.

A pillar under the two buttons, 0.1 mm below the board, takes a paperclip push.
The old printed peg at the antenna corner is gone, because there is no hole
near the antenna any more.

**The front wall is moved back over the board.** The USB-A mouths are only
0.5-0.6 mm past the board edge, and a plug's overmould must reach within about
0.5 mm of the mouth. A normal wall (board edge + 0.3 mm + 2 mm) would put the
mouths 1.7 mm behind the outer face, and plugs would not seat. So the front
wall's outer face is 0.4 mm in front of the mouths (0.3 mm by the board notes'
25.6), and it is a full 2 mm thick above the board (in the lid) and below it
(the ledge, in the base). The board's front edge sits in a slot in that wall.
Only the 0.6 mm skin directly in front of the board edge is thinner than 2 mm.
Each USB-A cut-out is the mouth flange (14.4 x 7.0 mm, measured from the STEP)
plus 0.5 mm all round. The ports are 17 mm apart, so the wall between two
notches is 1.6 mm wide.

**USB-A pins.** The shield legs of neighbouring ports are only 1.4 mm apart
under the board. So the floor has a single recess under all five ports
(y 10.8-19.5, full width), down to 1 mm below the 3 mm design pin length.

**USB-C**: the back wall is a normal 2 mm wall. Each receptacle passes through a
cut-out 0.5 mm larger than its body, and the outer face around it is recessed
13.5 x 8.0 mm (a 12.5 x 7 mm overmould + 0.5 mm) down to 0.4 mm outside the
mouth. The plug therefore seats fully. That leaves a 0.7 mm frame around each
receptacle.

**LED windows: Ø2.5 mm through-holes**, chosen over 0.4 mm thin spots because:
* they work in any colour, including the brand's black (Pitch), while a thin
  spot only glows in white or natural material;
* a 0.4 mm skin is just two FDM layers, which come out blotchy and pinholed, and
  it is a bloomed first layer in resin;
* each window runs from just above its LED through the heat pad and the top
  plate, so it only passes light from almost straight below, and a neighbour's
  colour does not leak through.

`LED_WINDOW = 'thin'` gives the thin-spot version for translucent material.

**Buttons: Ø3 mm paperclip holes**, reasons:
* they print reliably on FDM and resin;
* RESET cannot be pressed by accident on a hub that powers printers.

The pad over each button stays 1 mm above the plunger and 1 mm clear of the
button body, as the old guide tubes did, so the clip lands on the plunger.

**Antenna.** No screw, insert or vent is within 10 mm of the ESP32's antenna
area (x 23.2-42.8, y -25.0 to -18.4). The nearest screw is 19.9 mm away and
the nearest vent 10.4 mm.

**Vents**:
* 5 top slots (1.6 x 14 mm) right over the buck converter and its inductor,
  through the heat pad;
* 6 slots in the left wall beside the buck;
* 5 slots in the right wall, away from the antenna, for cross-flow.

The side slots run from the split plane up and are open at the lid's edge, so
they print without bridges. They continue 2 mm into the heat pad so that they
open into the air under it.

**Brand.** The outline is all right angles, with no fillets and no chamfers
(none are needed to print; use the slicer's elephant-foot compensation instead).
Text is engraved 0.6 mm deep in JetBrains Mono 500 with 0.06 em tracking, from
`print-hub/v1/fonts`:
* port numbers 1-5 above the ports (7 mm);
* POWER and DATA by the USB-Cs (4 mm, down from 4.6 to clear the back-left
  screw; POWER sits 1 mm right of its port for the same reason);
* RST and BOOT to the right of their holes (3.6 mm), since the screw is on the left;
* PRINT HUB centred between that screw and the port LED windows.

The fit check keeps every text at least 1 mm from every opening, from the
other texts and from the lid edge. The labels are uppercase as requested. The
brand would set word labels in Instrument Sans, sentence case. The glyph
outlines are merged with shapely, because the variable font's overlapping
contours break OCCT's own text builder.

## Heat

With 8.4 mm of still air above the board and 4 mm below, the board ran too hot
at 3 A in a 35 °C room (simulated: hottest capacitor 96 °C, limit 85 °C). The
case now brings its inner surfaces close to the board (simulated: 80 °C):

* **Heat pads.** The lid's underside follows the parts. Over every point of the
  board it sits 0.6 mm above the tallest part within 0.5 mm of that point (each
  courtyard grown by 0.5 mm, by 1 mm round the USB-A shells and the buttons).
  It is never lower than 1.2 mm above the bare board and never higher than the
  old ceiling. The USB-A receptacles keep the full ceiling (1.05 mm of air
  over them, as before).
* **Raised floor.** The floor sits 1.0 mm under the board, except for the
  recess under the USB-A pins. The board's bottom has no parts.
* **Feet.** The printed feet hold the floor 3.5 mm off the desk, so its outer
  face is in free air.

`case_gaps.json` gives the gaps for `sim/thermal/board_heat.py`. Its
rectangles are the same ones the model is built from. Units are mm, board
centre origin, +y towards the front:
* `top_default_mm` 1.2 and `bottom_default_mm` 1.0;
* `top` and `bottom`: rectangles with their gap. The gap at a point is the
  largest of the default and every rectangle that contains it;
* `outside_mm`, `wall_mm`, `feet_mm`.

Contacts are not in the file: the standoffs, the lid's bosses, the front ledge
and the front pads, and the button pillar (0.1 mm). The format cannot express
a gap below the default there, so the file is slightly pessimistic.

| Gap | mm |
|---|---|
| Lid underside above the bare board | 1.2 |
| ... above 0402 / 0603 / 1206 shunts / QFN / WSON / LEDs | 1.2-1.5 |
| ... above SOT-23, SOD-123, 1206 capacitors | 1.8-2.2 |
| ... above the buttons | 2.5 |
| ... above the TVS, inductor, ESP32, USB-C | 3.0-3.85 |
| ... above the USB-A strip | 8.4 |
| Floor below the board | 1.0 |
| ... under the USB-A pins | 4.0 |

## Fit check result

`enclosure.py` builds simple boxes for the board and its parts from the measured
STEP sizes, takes every other part's courtyard from the board file, and uses the
real STEP models of the USB-A and USB-C receptacles. It then checks:
* that nothing intersects the base or lid;
* that the lid is 0.6 mm above every part and the floor is 1 mm under the board;
* plug seating and mouth setback;
* cut-out clearance and heights;
* wall thickness, probed through the solids;
* the fasteners and vents against the antenna rule, and the front-edge clamp;
* the lid's top face: openings and engraved text;
* that the STLs are watertight and the feet are there;
* the overhangs in print orientation.

The board is the only thing that touches the enclosure, where it is clamped.

## Things to check on the first print

* **USB-A mouth position.** The STEP (matched to the footprint's pad rows) puts
  the mouth at y = 25.5. The board notes and the courtyard say 25.6. The wall
  face is at 25.9, so the setback is 0.3-0.4 mm either way. Confirm a plug seats
  fully. `MOUTH_SETBACK` moves the wall.
* **USB-A height.** The board's own 3D model (`../print-hub-step.zip`) places
  the receptacle 0.35 mm lower than this script's reading of the same STEP
  (its top 7.0 mm above the board instead of 7.35, its legs 2.75 mm below
  instead of 2.4). Both fit. In the board's version, the mouth flange is
  0.15 mm above the bottom of the notch.
* **Part heights.** `PART_HEIGHTS` uses the heat brief's figures, raised where
  the board's 3D model is taller. The real board model is checked against the
  case by `fit_board.py`.
* **Through-hole pins.** The floor recess is designed for 3.0 mm pins with 1 mm
  to spare. Trim the leads if they come out longer.
* **Plug sizes.** The checks assume a 16 x 8 mm USB-A overmould and a 12.5 x 7 mm
  USB-C overmould. Fatter USB-A plugs still stop at the wall face, 0.4 mm from
  the mouth. Plugs over 17 mm wide cannot sit side by side at the 17 mm port
  pitch anyway.
* **Front clamp.** The six pads on the front strip are small (1.3-1.6 x 1.1 mm).
  After tightening the screws, press the front corners: the board must not move.
* **Insert pocket.** Ø4.0 mm suits most M3 x 4 inserts. Adjust `INSERT_HOLE_D` to yours.
* **Board thickness.** With a 1.6 ± 0.16 mm board, a thick board leaves a hairline
  gap at the split, and a thin one gets up to 0.16 mm of play before the walls meet.
* **Heat.** Check the buck area and the lid over it after an hour at full load.

## Fit against the real board

`fit_board.py` loads the assembled board's STEP (`../print-hub-step.zip`,
every part's model on its land) and the case's STEP and checks that no
solid of the board overlaps the base, the lid or the feet. Result on the
86 x 50 board (18 Oct 2026): **PASS**, 368 board solids against 6 case
solids, no overlap. Closest approach, measured on the real models: 0.6 mm
from any part to the heat pads (the SOT-23 amplifiers); 0.4-0.5 mm only at
the walls and cut-outs (ESP32 module to the back wall, USB-A and USB-C in
their cut-outs); the USB-A mouth flanges 0.15 mm above the notch bottoms
(see "USB-A height"). The USB-A receptacles are the tallest parts, 7.0 mm
above the board.

cd print-hub/v1 && tools/kd python3 mechanical/enclosure/fit_board.py
