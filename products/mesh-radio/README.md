# Mesh Radio

**A LoRa mesh radio on one narrow board, built only from parts a US or
allied factory can buy and assemble.** It does what the RAK WisBlock does in
OffGrid's mesh nodes today (Meshtastic over LoRa, set up from the phone over
Bluetooth), on a single board shaped like a Heltec V4. It also has a
transmit amplifier that goes past the legal 1 W for licensed or experimental
use, a locking serial port for the Cairn drone, and protection on every
input.

> **Status: re-routed, DRC 0/0/0, fab files made, every simulation re-run
> on the routed board (5 Nov 2026). Not yet built.**
>
> - **Board:** 70 × 25.4 mm, 6 layers, an M3 hole in each corner, every cable plug on one long edge (JLCPCB JLC06161H-2116B: In1 solid
>   ground under the radio lines and the antenna, signals elsewhere on In1–In4 with ground fill). 257 parts, 251 of them
>   assembled, on both sides.
> - **Routed.** KiCad DRC 0 errors, 0 warnings, 0 unconnected; copper nets
>   equal the circuit pad for pad. Gerbers, BOM, CPL, assembly drawing and
>   STEP are in [`production/`](production/) and [`mechanical/`](mechanical/);
>   renders in [`images/`](images/).
> - **Simulations** (power, radio, heat) re-ran on the re-routed board
>   (5 Nov). Still failing: transmitting above 1 W in a hot box (the heat
>   study's duty table). The antenna switch's overload with the antenna
>   missing at 4.0 V is handled by a firmware ceiling (checked by
>   simulation only). Results: [`TESTS.md`](TESTS.md).
> - **Firmware:** the Meshtastic variant builds. Nothing has run on
>   hardware.
> - **Owner decisions, 19 Oct:** keep 3 W and fix the heat (a gap pad to a
>   metal enclosure, nine filled vias under the amplifier); 6 layers; the
>   designer picks the new parts; charging at 300 mA.
>
> Nothing is ordered or measured. Every test and its result:
> [`TESTS.md`](TESTS.md).

## Who it is for

- **Cairn nodes.** These are the mesh nodes the Cairn drone drops: on a
  1.5–2 m pole or on a treetop.
  - Each node weighs under 300 g.
  - A node runs on one LiPo cell for days to weeks.
  - In the sun, it sits in a sealed box at 55–60 °C.
  - The node is live on the mesh while the drone carries it. The drone's
    onboard computer (Jetson Orin Nano) reads signal strength from it over
    a cable to pick the drop point.
- **Any OffGrid node** that uses a RAK WisBlock today.
- **A defence build later.** If a contract needs NDAA-compliant radios built
  in the US or allied countries, this design is the starting point. It
  should need only minor changes, not months of work.

## Testing: what can and cannot be proven before building

Proven in simulation before ordering:

- power surges and plug-in spikes;
- short circuits and reversed battery;
- weak, bad and over-voltage batteries;
- solar input;
- 3 W bursts on a worn battery;
- heat at 60 °C with a message storm;
- filter harmonics;
- trace impedance.

Proven only on built boards:

- the exact output power;
- antenna matching;
- receive sensitivity;
- real current draw.

The plan is a small test batch measured on a bench with a spectrum
analyser, side by side with a RAK. The full list, with each result so far:
[`TESTS.md`](TESTS.md).

1. **Before power:** look for solder bridges at the 0.4 mm WLCSPs (U6,
   U14, the buck), the nRF52840 and the SX1262. With a meter, check that
   3V3, VSYS and BAT_P are not shorted to ground, and **that J3's pad 1 is
   + on a real Adafruit cell's plug** (requirement P3: the footprint
   drawing says so; nobody has measured it).
2. **First power from a current-limited bench supply**, not a cell: 3.8 V
   on the battery pads, limit 100 mA, power switch on. The board should
   draw a few mA with no firmware; the test pads should read 3.3 V on 3V3
   and the supply on VSYS. Then USB-C alone: the red charge LED behaves as
   the charger's status pin says.
3. **Bootloader over SWD**, once per board (the SWDIO / SWDCLK / reset test
   pads, steps in `firmware/README.md`, *Flash*). Then a double-press of
   reset brings up the `OGMESHBOOT` drive.
4. **Meshtastic:** copy the `.uf2` from the build (`firmware/README.md`,
   *Build*) onto `OGMESHBOOT`. The board reboots into Meshtastic; pair it
   from the phone app over Bluetooth, set the US region (1 W) first.
5. **First radio checks, with the antenna on:** send at the US setting into
   a 30 dB attenuator and a power meter (about 29 dBm expected; the gain
   table is a model, `TESTS.md` hardware rows); receive side by side with
   a RAK node. Watch the log for `ANTENNA MISSING?` lines: there should be
   none with a good antenna.
6. **The missing-antenna check,** only after the above, at the US setting
   first: unplug the antenna and send once. The log should show
   `ANTENNA MISSING?` and the amplifier off; plug it back, and the retry
   (a 1 W soft start, 30 s later) brings full power back. Do not test
   above 1 W without the antenna (*Limits*).
7. **Above 1 W only with the gap pad** on a metal wall and a known-good
   antenna, and record the case temperature against `sim/thermal`.

## Limits — read these

- **Not yet built.** Every result is a simulation or a check of the design
  files. Nothing has been measured.
- **Heat without the gap pad:** at 25 °C, 1 W passes at 25 % duty; 2.5 W and
  3 W pass at no duty (not even one 1 s message a minute); in a 60 °C box
  nothing passes, because the cell's area passes 60 °C with the radio only
  listening (`sim/thermal`, 5 Nov).
- **The gap pad to a metal wall is what makes power above 1 W usable:** with
  it, 1 W runs continuously at 25 °C and 35 dBm passes one message a minute
  (34 dBm in a 25 % storm). The pad sits just below the plugs' row, 4.3 mm
  from the amplifier, not under it (the plugs are there).
- **Antenna switch with the antenna missing:** at a 4.0 V amplifier supply
  (the opt-in `OFFGRID_PA_ALLOW_4V0` build) the SKY13330 sees 109 % of its
  rating. The firmware holds the supply at 3.6 V (98 %) after the first
  antenna fault until restart, but the packet that finds the antenna missing
  runs at its own level for up to 750 µs first. Simulation only.
- **The harmonic filter's notches** sit at 1.42 / 2.44 GHz, not on 2f and
  3f: marginal by design. The LFCG-1000+ filter carries the rejection
  (21.7 dB margin at 35 dBm, 6 dBi).
- **The transmit-loss spread is an assumption:** 82 % of simulated boards
  lose more than 1.3 dB (3 W at 4.0 V needs ≤ 1.3 dB), but that spread is
  the LFCG-1000+ and SKY13330 losses taken as ×1.0–1.5 and typical-to-max;
  with them at typical no board loses more than 1.26 dB. Measure it.
- **The TPS63810 buck-boost cannot carry a 3 W burst in a hot box.** During
  a 3 W burst it dissipates about 1 W: 204 % of its rated dissipation at
  85 °C ambient and 114 % at 60 °C (`sim/derating`, listed as OVER,
  owner-accepted). The heat model puts its junction at 152 °C (85 °C) and
  127 °C (60 °C) after a 1 s burst a minute, over its 125 °C rating
  (`sim/thermal`). It is safe only because the firmware never does that:
  its temperature cap, read on the board beside the amplifier, allows 2 W
  above 45 °C, 1 W above 60 °C (0.3 W in the buck-boost, 34–60 % of its
  rating) and turns the amplifier off above 75 °C. So "3 W at 85 °C" in the
  burst study is the cell and the supply's limit, not a working mode; 3 W is
  a below-45 °C mode. At 25 °C, 3 W one message a minute leaves the
  junction at 93 °C.
- **Battery to the power mux is 83 mΩ of copper** (BAT_P on inner layers,
  15 mm, since the battery plug moved away from the amplifier for heat).
  3 W bursts still pass from a fresh cell; it costs headroom on a weak one.
- **Bluetooth match and harmonic filter values are fitted to the routed
  lines in a model,** not measured; the antenna is Johanson's EVB model,
  not an EM model of this board. They are the starting point for the
  network-analyser tune.
- **The firmware compiles but has never run** (Meshtastic v2.7.26 with the
  core patch, 6 Nov). The power table, the antenna-detector thresholds and
  the battery cap table are models until measured.
- **Listening current is about 12–13 mA** with the receive amplifier on
  (requirement P1 asks 10 mA); battery-saver bypass gets about 8 mA.
- **The 0805 inductors are not stocked at JLCPCB** (*Ordering*).

## Build

Everything runs in the pinned toolchain image (`tools/Dockerfile`, KiCad
10.0.7), from this folder:

```
tools/kd python3 src/make.py              # gates and every output from the committed board
tools/kd python3 src/make.py --artwork    # lay the silkscreen out again first
tools/kd python3 src/sch.py               # the schematic, ERC and its PDF
tools/ci/local.sh products/mesh-radio     # CI, from the repository root, as GitHub runs it
```

The committed `mesh-radio.kicad_pcb` is the design of record: CI (`ci /
mesh-radio`) rebuilds every fab file from it and fails if they differ from
what is committed.

**Simulations** (each writes its `results.txt` beside it; `TESTS.md` has the
latest result of each):

```
tools/kd python3 sim/rf/routed.py && tools/kd python3 sim/rf/run.py   # radio, ~5 min
NPROC=2 tools/kd python3 sim/thermal/run.py                          # heat, ~30 min
NPROC=2 tools/kd python3 sim/thermal/followup.py                     # heat per board option, ~40 min
tools/kd python3 sim/extract.py     # the routed board's copper -> copy the values into sim/board.py
tools/kd sh sim/run_all.sh          # power: hotplug, reverse, shorts, burst, solar, derating (~3 h on 4 cores)
```

### How the board was routed

The committed copper came from `src/pipeline.py` on `src/placement.json`
(re-routed 5 Nov for the radio, heat and power findings of the routed-board
studies; `git log` has each change and its reason):

```
tools/kd python3 src/pipeline.py <work> --passes 25       # ~1.5 h on 4 cores: 4 open, 23 DRC errors
tools/kd python3 src/pipeline.py <work> --local-repair    # run four times: 0 open, 0 errors, 6 warnings
tools/kd python3 src/pipeline.py <work> --from-clean      # 0 errors, 0 warnings, 0 unconnected
```

What the pipeline does, in order:

- **Board** (`src/board.py`): parts at `placement.json`; the RF lines, the
  cell protector's sense loop (FETs straight into R34 on B.Cu, 0.6 mm) and
  the SX1262's VR_PA escape (pin 24 up beside RFO's escape) are drawn and
  locked; In1 solid under the RF lines and the antenna.
- **Fan-out**: ball escapes; a ground via per ground pad, its own for each
  RF shunt and each of the harmonic filter's ground pads.
- **First pass, alone, locked** (`FIRST_NETS`): the SX1262 bus and the short
  nets that otherwise ended boxed in (U6's BYP_OUT/BYP_ILIM, LORA_DIO2,
  LORA_VRPA, MUX_SS, MUX_ILM).
- **Freerouting** (25 passes), then other nets' pieces closer than 0.25 mm
  to an RF line are taken out (`rf_gap_strip`: Freerouting keeps each class's
  own clearance, not the larger).
- **Tail**: the maze, repairs, power nets poured round their own copper
  (`power_pours`), ground pours and stitching, clean-up.
- **`--local-repair`** (each run from the tail's saved input): parts whose
  `placement.json` place differs are moved on the routed board
  (`apply_moves`); the pours refilled and KiCad's own open list taken with
  the pipeline's; a via beside every lone pad of an open net
  (`lone_pads`); the local rip-up repair; a via for every ground pad with
  none within 0.6 mm (`ground_pads`); the tail again.
- **`--from-clean`**: hole pass, clean-up, dangling stubs taken out or cut
  back to their joint (`prune_dangling`), POFV spacing, stackup, DRC.

The code changed between the repair runs of 5 Nov (each fix is a commit);
the whole recipe has not been run again from scratch on the final code.
`make.py --reroute` runs only the first step.

### The schematic

```
tools/kd python3 src/sch.py     # from products/mesh-radio, in the pinned KiCad 10.0.7 image
```

It redraws `schematic/` from `src/circuit.py`, checks that KiCad's netlist
equals the circuit, runs KiCad's ERC and exports the PDF. Rebuilds are
byte-identical.
